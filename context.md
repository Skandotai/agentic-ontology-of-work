# Rationale

A summary of why AOW was developed. The full argument is in the [whitepaper](whitepaper.md), also available as a [PDF](docs/downloads/agentic-ontology-of-work-v2.0.pdf).

## From terminology to structure

The [SOA-to-Agentic AI Terminology Mapping](https://github.com/Skandotai/soa-to-agentic-terms) established a shared vocabulary for agentic systems by adapting terms from Service-Oriented Architecture. A shared vocabulary does not by itself give organizations a shared structure. Two teams can agree on what an Agent and an Intent are and still model the relationship between them differently, which prevents their systems from exchanging a consistent record of what was done and why.

SOA addressed the same issue with a formal ontology, first published by The Open Group and later standardized as ISO/IEC 18384-3. AOW is intended to serve the same purpose for agentic work.

## What an ontology adds

A glossary defines terms. An ontology also specifies:

- **Attributes.** For example, an Intent has a goal statement, a priority, an acceptable risk band, and a required Assurance Level.
- **Relationships.** For example, an Intent serves an Objective, is shaped by Context, is constrained by Policy, and is realized by a Plan of Tasks.
- **Constraints.** For example, every Result is generated by an Action, and every Action is performed by an Actor.

Because these are specified formally, conformance can be verified by software. This allows an organization to demonstrate, rather than assert, that its agents operate under defined governance.

## Requirements specific to agentic work

Services execute fixed operations. Agents interpret goals, plan, and act with varying degrees of autonomy. An ontology of agentic work therefore needs concepts that SOA did not:

- **Graded autonomy.** AOW defines five levels, from AL0 (work performed by people) to AL4 (agents act without routine review), and a rule for determining the applicable level when an agent, a task, and a tool specify different levels.
- **Confidence.** An agent may act independently when its confidence meets the required threshold and must escalate when it does not.
- **Human participation.** Approvals and decisions made by people are recorded in the same form as actions taken by agents.
- **Feedback.** Outcomes and reviews produce Feedback that updates Policies, Skills, and Memory, and these updates are recorded.

## Open questions

AOW describes work within a single organization's governance. The following remain open: how autonomy, trust, and policy apply when agents from different organizations collaborate; how to model negotiation between agents; and how to ensure that confidence values are calibrated. The whitepaper lists these in section 15.
